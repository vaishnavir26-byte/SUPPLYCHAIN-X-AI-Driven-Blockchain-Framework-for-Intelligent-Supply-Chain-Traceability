02 • RESEARCH SUMMARY SLIDE 03 / 28
Abstract in One Slide

"Can target-aware allocation increase the number of users meeting a required data rate without hiding the costs in throughput or fairness?"

Experimental Campaign

  • • 4 User Populations: 5, 10, 20, 50 users
  • • 3 Target Rates: 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 Mbps
  • • 10 Random Seeds: 42–51 (paired across methods)
  • • 4 Methods Evaluated: Equal, Greedy, PF, V2
  • • Total Evaluations: 4 × 12 × 10 = 480 evaluations

Headline Findings

Reliability Improvement Improved in 8 out of 12 scenarios

Gains reach up to +25.8 percentage points under high congestion (50 users).

The Trade-Off (Academic Honesty) Lower Jain fairness in all 12 scenarios

To ensure target compliance, V2 discriminates across users, reducing rate uniformity.

Deterministic comparative study • All 480 runs passed programmed resource budget validation
03 • MOTIVATION SLIDE 04 / 28

Why Resource Allocation Is Difficult

Users compete for limited spectrum and power, but their channel conditions and distances are highly heterogeneous.

Physical Constraints

  • • Finite Bandwidth: $B_{\max} = 10.0\text{ MHz}$
  • • Finite Transmit Power: $P_{\max} = 1.0\text{ W}$
  • • Heterogeneous channel power gains ($g_i$)
  • • Thermal noise ($N_i$) and interference ($I_i$)

Competing Objectives

  • • Sum Throughput: Maximize total bits transmitted
  • • Reliability: Ensure users meet required $R_t$
  • • Fairness: Avoid starving cell-edge users
  • • Power Efficiency: Stay within radiation caps

Design Implication

Evaluating wireless networks on throughput alone is dangerously misleading. A single high-rate user can mask an unacceptable outage rate among 49 other users.

ComAgent evaluates throughput, reliability, minimum rate, and Jain fairness side-by-side.
Network trade-off: Capacity maximization vs Quality-of-Service guarantees

07 • REFERENCE DETAIL SLIDE 08 / 28

Kelly et al. (1998): Why Proportional Fair Is Included

Connecting rate control, shadow prices, and proportional fairness utility

Core Mathematical Objective

$$\text{maximize} \quad \sum_{i=1}^N \log(r_i)$$

Logarithmic utility provides decreasing marginal returns: giving an extra Mbps to a starving user yields vastly more utility than giving it to an already fast user.

Research Audit Note

In our reported simulation dataset, the Proportional Fair implementation converged to identical numerical summaries as Equal Allocation across multiple seeds.

Viva Honesty: Rather than hiding this, we openly flag that the PF baseline requires an optimization step-size audit before claiming a distinct empirical PF curve.
Comparator foundation: Kelly et al. provides the conceptual benchmark for fairness-throughput trade-off.
08 • REFERENCE DETAIL SLIDE 09 / 28

Jain et al. (1984): How Fairness Is Measured

A fairness metric is required because aggregate throughput can hide severe starvation for weaker users.

Jain's Fairness Index Formula

$$J(r) = \frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^N r_i\right)^2}{N \sum_{i=1}^N r_i^2}$$
  • • Theoretical Range: $[\frac{1}{N}, 1.0]$
  • • Closer to 1.0: User rates are completely equal
  • • Lower Value: Allocation is skewed / uneven

Usage in ComAgent

  • • Evaluated directly on the achieved user rate vector $r$ after each simulation run
  • • Reported alongside Throughput, Reliability, and Minimum Rate
  • • Prevents drawing superficial conclusions based purely on throughput gains
  • • Core Finding: V2 has lower Jain fairness than Equal allocation in all 12 scenarios
Metric reference: Jain et al. supplies the evaluation standard; it does not define the scheduling policy itself.

Converting assigned spectrum, power, and channel states into achieved user bit rates.

$$r_i = B_i \log_2\left(1 + \frac{P_i g_i}{N_i + I_i}\right)$$
$B_i$

Portion of 10 MHz budget

$P_i$

Assigned Power

Portion of 1.0 W budget

$g_i$

Channel Gain

Pathloss + Rayleigh fading

$N_i + I_i$

Noise & Interference

Bandwidth-scaled thermal noise

Simplified mathematical abstraction for comparative simulation, not calibrated physical transceiver testing.
10 • REFERENCE DETAIL SLIDE 11 / 28

Efron & Tibshirani: Why Paired Bootstrap Is Used

Because identical channel seeds are paired across methods, the analysis computes matched per-seed differences $\Delta = \text{V2} - \text{Equal}$.

Step 1: Pair

For each seed $k \in \{42..51\}$, compute the exact matched difference:

$\Delta_k = R_{\text{V2}}(k) - R_{\text{Eq}}(k)$
Step 2: Resample

Draw samples with replacement from the paired differences vector of size 10.

Step 3: Repeat

Run $B = 20,000$ independent bootstrap iterations for robust distributional tails.

Step 4: Interval

Extract empirical 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles to report the 95% Confidence Interval.

ComAgent Parameters: 10 paired seeds per scenario • Analysis RNG Seed: 20261029 • 95% Percentile CI
Avoids assuming asymptotic normality on small sample sizes (N=10 seeds).

Inputs, Decision Variables & Constraints

The allocator chooses per-user bandwidth and power allocations while strictly adhering to system budgets.

System Inputs

  • • $N$: Number of users $\{5, 10, 20, 50\}$
  • • $R_t$: Required data rate $\{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}\text{ Mbps}$
  • • $g_i$: Channel power gain vector

  • • $I_i$: Modeled co-channel interference

Decision Variables

  • • $B_i \ge 0$: Bandwidth assigned to user $i$
  • • $P_i \ge 0$: Power assigned to user $i$

Continuous non-negative domain with discrete chunk reallocation in heuristic step.

$$\sum_{i=1}^N B_i \le 10.0\text{ MHz}$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^N P_i \le 1.0\text{ W}$$
$$B_i \ge 0, \quad P_i \ge 0, \quad \forall i$$

Deterministic validation ensures zero physical resource over-commitment.

14 • PROPOSED MODEL SNAPSHOT SLIDE 15 / 28

Reliability Aware V2: Decision Flow

Transitioning from blind symmetric splitting to a target-aware, feasible-first allocation policy:

1. Estimate Need

Under baseline power, compute the exact bandwidth $\hat{B}_i$ needed for user $i$ to hit target rate $R_t$:

$\hat{B}_i = \frac{R_t}{\log_2(1 + \text{SINR}_i)}$
2. Sort & Prioritize

Order users ascending by resource need. Users requiring the least bandwidth to become reliable are served first.

3. Feasible-First

Commit bandwidth sequentially while remaining budget allows. Stop when budget cannot satisfy the next user.

4. Greedy Surplus

Any leftover spectral or power chunks are greedily assigned to maximize aggregate sum-throughput.

Theoretical Characterization: V2 is an engineering heuristic designed for target satisfaction; it is not guaranteed to be Pareto optimal across both fairness and throughput.
Target-aware prioritization converts wasted headroom into guaranteed connections for borderline users.
15 • MODEL IMPROVEMENT SLIDE 16 / 28

Old Baseline vs Proposed V2

A rigorous comparison between the project's Equal Allocation baseline and Reliability Aware V2:

OLD MODEL: Equal Allocation BASELINE
  • 1. Divide total spectrum equally: $B_i = B_{\max} / N$
  • 2. Divide total power equally: $P_i = P_{\max} / N$
  • 3. Every user receives identical resource slice regardless of conditions

Strength: Simple, predictable, maximal Jain fairness.

Limitation: Blind to channel variations; weak users fail to reach $R_t$.

NEW MODEL: Reliability Aware V2 PROPOSED
  • 1. Estimate bandwidth required per user to hit target rate $R_t$
  • 2. Prioritize users requiring the least bandwidth to become feasible
  • 3. Allocate feasible requirements first until budget is exhausted
  • 4. Distribute surplus resources greedily

Strength: Maximizes probability of meeting target rate.

Trade-off: Jain fairness drops significantly.

Design paradigm shift: Equal resource distribution → Target-aware feasibility prioritization

17 • IMPLEMENTATION EXPLAINED SLIDE 18 / 28

How User Data Rates Are Calculated & Interpreted

Translating assigned resources into rate and deriving the 4 primary network evaluation metrics:

Key Factors Driving User Rate

  • • Assigned Bandwidth ($B_i$): Linearly expands transmission capacity
  • • Allocated Power ($P_i$): Boosts signal strength above the noise floor
  • • Channel Quality ($g_i$): Distance and multipath fading attenuation
  • • Noise & Modeled Interference ($N_i + I_i$): Thermal floor plus inter-cell coupling

How Results Are Interpreted

  • • Throughput ($T$): Sum of all user achieved rates $\sum r_i$
  • • Reliability ($R$): Percentage of users with achieved rate $r_i \ge R_t$
  • • Minimum Rate: Lowest rate received by any user (starvation check)
  • • Jain Fairness: Degree of rate equality across the user population
Multi-dimensional evaluation prevents optimizing one metric at the secret expense of another.
18 • IMPLEMENTATION EXPLAINED SLIDE 19 / 28

How the Four Allocation Methods Differ

Each method operates under identical network conditions but makes distinct resource-allocation trade-offs:

Equal Allocation

Resource Equality

Splits spectrum and power evenly ($B/N, P/N$). Predictable, but blind to differing channel conditions and target thresholds.

Greedy Allocation

Sum-Rate Maximization

Assigns resources to the user with the cleanest channel to maximize aggregate sum-rate. Leaves cell-edge users starved.

Proportional Fair

Log-Utility Balance

Balances sum-rate and relative fairness using logarithmic utility $\sum \log(r_i)$. Avoids optimizing only the sum-rate.

Reliability Aware V2

Target Compliance

Estimates required resources to hit $R_t$, prioritizes feasible users, and redistributes remainder greedily.

Distinct philosophical priorities: Equality vs Raw Speed vs Utility vs Reliability.

Does V2 Improve Reliability in Every Scenario?

No. The outcome depends heavily on user density and spectral congestion:

8 / 12
Scenarios Improved

In congested networks (10, 20, 50 users), V2 consistently outperforms Equal Allocation, reaching gains of up to +25.8 pp.

1 / 12
Scenario Unchanged

At 5 users and 1.0 Mbps target, both methods reached identical reliability outcomes (+0.0 pp delta).

3 / 12
Scenarios Decreased

All 3 decreases occurred strictly at 5 users, where Equal Allocation already has abundant spectrum and near-perfect reliability.

Crucial Observation: In all 12 scenarios without exception, V2 exhibited lower Jain fairness than Equal Allocation.
Scientific takeaway: Target-aware scheduling is effective under congestion, but unnecessary in lightly loaded networks.

24 • LIMITATIONS & INTEGRITY SLIDE 25 / 28

What the Current Evidence Does Not Prove

Honest scientific boundaries that must be stated during a defense or viva examination:

Model & Algorithmic Limits

  • • Shannon Abstraction: Does not model HARQ, Doppler shift, or channel estimation errors.
  • • Sample Size: 10 random seeds per scenario is a preliminary exploration.
  • • Heuristic Nature: V2 is an engineering greedy-heuristic, not a provably optimal Pareto frontier solver.
  • • PF Implementation Audit: PF matched Equal in reported runs; requires gradient step audit.

Evidence & Agent Layer Limits

  • • Budget Checks: 480 validation checks verify programmed budget caps only.
  • • LLM Selector API Rate Limit: Automated LLM agent tests hit external API quotas; present findings evaluate the deterministic core heuristics.
  • • No Unverified AI Claims: We claim no autonomous LLM speedup or token cost advantage at this stage.
  • • Future Work: Link-level 5G NR validation and expanding to 100+ seeds.
A credible research defense states both the measured achievement and the boundaries of the experiment.
25 • CONCLUSION & REFERENCES SLIDE 26 / 28

Final Research Conclusion

Reliability Aware V2 successfully improves network reliability in 8 out of 12 scenarios, demonstrating gains up to +25.8 percentage points under high user density. However, Jain fairness is lower than Equal Allocation in all 12 scenarios. Therefore, V2 is a reliability-focused engineering trade-off—not a universal drop-in replacement.
