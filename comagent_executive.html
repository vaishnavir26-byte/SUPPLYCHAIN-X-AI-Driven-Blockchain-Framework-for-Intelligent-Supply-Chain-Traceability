PROBLEM STATEMENT 02 / 10

The Outage Dilemma in Shared Spectrum

The Traditional Flaw

Most schedulers maximize aggregate sum-rate ($\sum r_i$). Under scarce resources, this gives almost all bandwidth to users with the cleanest channels.

Result: High aggregate speed, but severe outage for cell-edge users who fail their target rates ($R_t$).

ComAgent's Objective

Distribute limited spectrum ($10\text{ MHz}$) and power ($1.0\text{ W}$) to maximize the fraction of users meeting their target operating rate ($r_i \ge R_t$).

Focus: Quantify the explicit trade-off between reliability gains and Jain fairness.
Physical limits: 10 MHz spectrum • 1.0 W total transmit power • Heterogeneous channel gains ($g_i$)
MATHEMATICAL MODEL 03 / 10

Rate Abstraction & Fairness Metrics

Shannon Achievable Rate

$$r_i = B_i \log_2\left(1 + \frac{P_i g_i}{N_i + I_i}\right)$$
  • • $B_i$: Assigned bandwidth slice ($\sum B_i \le 10\text{ MHz}$)
  • • $P_i$: Assigned transmit power ($\sum P_i \le 1.0\text{ W}$)
  • • $N_i$: Thermal noise floor ($N_0 B_i$)
  • • $I_i$: Cross-user interference coupling

Jain's Fairness Index

$$J(r) = \frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^N r_i\right)^2}{N \sum_{i=1}^N r_i^2}$$
  • • Range: $[1/N, 1.0]$
  • • Evaluated directly on achieved rate vector $r$
  • • Closer to 1.0 = equal sharing; lower = skewed
  • • Prevents drawing false throughput-only conclusions
Theoretical grounding: Shannon (1948), Goldsmith (2005), and Jain et al. (1984).

PROPOSED METHOD 05 / 10

Reliability Aware V2: Feasible-First Allocation

1. Estimate Need

Inverts the rate formula to estimate bandwidth $\hat{B}_i$ needed for user $i$ to hit target rate $R_t$.

2. Sort by Need

Ranks users in ascending order of resource need. Users closest to feasibility are served first.

3. Feasible-First

Allocates bandwidth sequentially until budget runs out; prunes unserviceable users in this pass.

4. Greedy Surplus

Any leftover spectral or power headroom is greedily assigned to boost aggregate sum throughput.

Design shift: Blind symmetric division (Equal) $\rightarrow$ Target-aware threshold optimization (V2).

CONCLUSION & VIVA SUMMARY 10 / 10

Final Research Conclusion

Reliability Aware V2 increases the probability of users meeting their required data rates in 8 out of 12 scenarios, achieving gains up to +25.8 percentage points under severe congestion. However, its Jain fairness is lower than Equal Allocation in all 12 scenarios. It represents a reliability-focused engineering trade-off—not a universal drop-in replacement.
